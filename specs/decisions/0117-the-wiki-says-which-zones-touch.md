# The wiki says which zones touch, and never where

## Status
Accepted

## Context
The travel graph is read off map labels ([ADR 0062](./0062-a-travel-graph-of-zone-lines.md)), so it
knows exactly what a mapmaker wrote down and nothing else. On a 590-file pack that leaves **146
borders drawn from one side only** and **212 destinations no map file answered to** — and, worse, whole
zones an audit shows as reachable in the game and isolated in the graph.

Some of it is unfixable from the maps. Four of the five zones the graph folds as duplicate drawings
([ADR 0111](./0111-one-zone-one-map-file.md)) have **more travel labels in the drawing that loses**,
and the drawings cannot be merged because their coordinate frames genuinely differ — the same
`to Innothule Swamp` is at `-1101, 3200` in `sro` and `342, 596` in `southro`. Ocean of Tears is the
worst of it: a hub of boats, portals and translocators whose kept drawing labels one succor point.

eqlwiki states the same fact from the other side. Every zone page carries an **Adjacent Zones** row,
written by people who play this server.

## Decision
**The wiki's Adjacent Zones is a second source for *reachability*, and never for distance.**

Precedence, stated once and in this order: **an exact map label beats the wiki beats everything else.**

 1. A destination a map label names, resolved exactly, is a border **with coordinates**. Nothing else
    touches one. A person standing in the zone drew that, and no other source can say where a crossing
    is.
 2. The wiki **adds** a border the maps never established. It never overrides, never moves anything,
    and contributes no position — so a route through one is priced by `UNKNOWN_CROSSING`, wears its
    `?`, and the aside marks the border `wiki`. That is the honest shape for a crossing nobody drew.
 3. Everything else — the near-miss pairing of a label nothing could place
    ([ADR 0115](./0115-a-border-one-side-could-not-name.md)) — runs **last**, and is strengthened by
    the step above: a wiki border makes its two zones claim each other, which is exactly the
    corroboration that pairing needs in order to afford its looser tiers.

A zone the server hasn't opened is refused here as it is everywhere: the wiki describes EverQuest, this
graph describes what is reachable today.

**Shipped, not fetched.** `zones/adjacency.generated.ts` is committed and regenerated by
`npm run zones:adjacency`, the same shape as the expansion table beside it, so a normal launch costs
the wiki nothing. The crawl itself is deliberately light: titles from one category listing, then
wikitext **fifty pages per request** — a full refresh of all 117 zones is three calls.

## Consequences
On the pack that prompted it: **8 borders added, 103 already known**. The agreement is as valuable as
the additions — 103 pairs where the maps and the wiki independently say the same thing is the best
evidence the graph is right that we have ever had. The additions include **East Freeport ↔ Ocean of
Tears**, one of the gaps that made that zone a mess.

The pairing pass got better for free: **17 → 19** labels placed, because the new borders gave it two
more corroborated candidates.

`one-sided` goes **146 → 154**, and that is the feature working rather than a regression: eight borders
that did not exist now exist, unplaced, flagged, and reachable.

**47 stated pairs name a zone this pack has no file for** and 2 name a zone out of era. Most are
Kunark. They are reported rather than forced.

Rejected:

- **Fetching at runtime.** A page per zone on every launch, against a community wiki, for data that
  changes about never.
- **Letting the wiki supply positions.** It has none to supply. Inventing one would turn a stand-in a
  route already flags into a number that looks measured.
- **Letting the wiki override a drawn border.** The mapmaker was there.
- **Merging duplicate drawings to recover their labels.** Their frames differ, so the coordinates would
  be nonsense; the wiki is the right way to get those connections back.
