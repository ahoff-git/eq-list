# 0122: A zone wears its levels, as the wiki wrote them

## Status
Accepted

## Context
The Hunt tab answers *where do I go to farm what's left*, and every zone it lists raises the same next
question: **can I survive there?** Nothing in the list could answer it. A hunt built from a level-8
character's shopping list will happily put Lower Guk and Greater Faydark in the same column, sorted by
how many needed items drop there, with nothing to say that one of them is a death.

eqlwiki already answers it. Every zone page's infobox carries a **Level of Monsters** row —
`1-12`, `4-15+`, `1-20, 35` — written by people who play this server. It is the same infobox, on the
same pages, that the adjacency table is read from
([ADR 0117](./0117-the-wiki-says-which-zones-touch.md)): the crawl exists, the pages are already
fetched, and this is one more row off them.

The rows are not tidy. Of the 116 zone pages, 99 state a level and the rest are cities that have no
monsters worth a number. Among the 99: two-band zones (`1-20, 35`), open-ended ones (`50+`), one with
its bands labelled by where they are (`29-34 Droga Main, 33-38 Inner Sanctum`), and three that answer
`?`, `n/a` or `Quest Only`.

## Decision
**A zone's stated levels are shown next to its name in the hunt, in the wiki's own wording.**

- **Verbatim, never resolved into a span.** `1-20, 35` collapsed to `1-35` asserts that Butcherblock
  holds levels 20 through 34, which is precisely what the wiki took the trouble not to say. Two bands
  and a gap is the fact; the min and the max are an invention. This is the same instinct as a mob's
  level one level down ([ADR 0121](./0121-a-mob-is-a-range-of-levels.md)) — a spread is the honest
  shape of "what level is this", and squeezing it loses the part worth reading. The only judgement
  applied is whether there is a digit in there at all: `?` and `Quest Only` say nothing a reader can
  use, and shown beside a zone name they would read as information.
- **Shipped, not fetched**, in `zones/levels.generated.ts`, regenerated by `npm run zones:levels`.
  A normal launch costs the wiki nothing, exactly as the expansion and adjacency tables beside it.
- **The crawl is shared, not copied.** `scripts/lib/eqlwiki.mjs` now owns the category listing, the
  fifty-pages-a-request wikitext fetch and the infobox row reader; the adjacency generator was moved
  onto it and produces its table byte for byte unchanged. A third row, when one is wanted, is a
  parser and a banner.
- **Looked up loosely, but not by spelling alone.** The names in hand come from item pages, map packs
  and the log, so `exact`, `order` and `typo` are all needed, and `narrower` with them. `fuzzy` is
  refused: this figure reads as *what you are walking into*, so the cost of matching the wrong zone
  is a person taking a level-8 character somewhere the wiki said 45. That is stricter than the
  expansion badge next door, which takes every tier because its worst case is a mislabel
  ([ADR 0068](./0068-a-zone-name-resolves-against-what-we-know.md)).
- **A page-title qualifier is not part of a zone's name.** `Cazic Thule (Zone)` disambiguates the
  zone from the god, and `(Pre-Revamp)`/`(Post-Revamp)` are two versions of one Chardok. A drop
  source says none of them, so they come off before matching — while the answer still names the page
  it came from, since the loose tiers mean it can differ from the name asked for.

## Consequences
99 of eqlwiki's 116 zones carry a range; the 17 that don't are the cities and the tutorial, which is
the right silence rather than a gap. A hunt zone the wiki has never heard of simply shows no range —
the same way it already shows no expansion badge.

The range is a **claim, not a measurement**, and it says so on hover: it is the zone's own page, not
our kills. Nothing reconciles it against what has actually been fought there. That is left open on
purpose — `mob-knowledge` records levels per mob, and comparing "the zone says 30-45" against "you
have conned five mobs here at 12" is a different feature, with a different failure mode.

`scripts/lib/cli.mjs` grew `writeGenerated`, the compare-report-write ending every generator in here
had its own copy of.

Rejected:

- **Parsing the row into `{ low, high }`.** It would fit `levels.ts`'s `LevelClaim` and be wrong for
  every multi-band zone in the table — see above.
- **Colouring the range against your own level** (green if you can take it, red if you can't). The
  app does not know your level reliably enough to gamble somebody's corpse on it, and a range with
  bands cannot be compared to a single number anyway.
- **Sorting or filtering the hunt by level.** The hunt is sorted by how much of your list a zone
  covers, which is what you asked it. Levels are context for that answer, not a competing one.
